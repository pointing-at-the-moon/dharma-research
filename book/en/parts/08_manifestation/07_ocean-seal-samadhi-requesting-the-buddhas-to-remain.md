## 8.7 The Ocean Seal Samādhi and Requesting the Buddhas to Remain in the World—Vow Seven Is Not Only Petition but Acknowledgment

The end of the previous chapter fixed the separation of space as an illusion. This chapter takes up the separation of time.

But its starting point is not a comfortable doctrinal deduction. It is a question of the sharpest kind—

**If the Ocean Seal Samādhi truly is the sealing-function of the dharma-realm manifesting moment by moment, then why did Śākyamuni Buddha enter *parinirvāṇa* between the twin trees at Kuśinagara?**

Śākyamuni Buddha's physical body did in fact pass away in history. His relics were divided. His disciples have guarded his teaching for more than two thousand years. These are unalterable historical facts.

And yet the seventh of Samantabhadra's ten great vows is called **requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world**. In the face of the historical fact that one Buddha has already entered *nirvāṇa* and that other Buddhas will enter it too, whom exactly does this vow ask to remain? What is it requesting?

This chapter answers that question. The answer, in a single sentence first: **requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world is not only a request that some external Buddha extend the lifespan of his physical body; it is also a direct, present acknowledgment of one structural fact—that the sealing-function of the Ocean Seal Samādhi "manifests moment by moment without cease."**

As regards Śākyamuni's physical body, petition is powerless before historical fact. Acknowledgment does not need historical fact to change.

### The Āgama Negative: The Rain-Drenched Night at the Cāpāla Shrine

To see this Huayan reversal clearly, we must first return to the āgamas—to a passage of scripture that has pained many readers.

The *Sūtra of the Great Decease* in the *Dīrgha-āgama* records a pivotal scene before the Buddha's passing. At the Cāpāla shrine near Vaiśālī, the Buddha said the same thing three times to Ānanda:

> Ānanda, whoever has cultivated the four bases of miraculous power, practiced them much, borne them in mind without forgetting, and set the will upon them—such a one may remain a kalpa or more. Ānanda, the Tathāgata has cultivated the four bases of miraculous power to the full, borne them in mind, set the will upon them: the Tathāgata is able to remain a kalpa or more, dispelling darkness for the world, bringing manifold benefit, giving peace to gods and humans.

Notice the structure of these words. The Buddha tells Ānanda plainly—**if someone requests it, he can remain a kalpa or more**. He is not lamenting his own imminent passing; he is opening a door.

The Buddha said this three times. **Three times Ānanda was silent.**

After the third utterance, receiving no response, the World-Honored One rebuked Ānanda sternly: "Ānanda, go. Know that it is now the time." After Ānanda departed, Māra Pāpīyas came and requested the Buddha to enter *nirvāṇa*, and the Buddha consented. When Ānanda returned and again requested the Buddha to remain, the Buddha said:

> What has already been cast off, already discharged—there is no place for the Tathāgata to contradict his own word. It is like a householder who has spat food upon the ground: will he take it up and eat it again? … Ānanda, the fault is precisely yours.

"Already cast off, already discharged." "The fault is precisely yours." The door of requesting once was missed, and never again could it be reached in time.

This is requesting-to-remain at the āgama level. It is a structural event-threshold—it can be requested, but the time is bounded; once missed, it is lost forever. This structure of the āgama is clear, severe, historical. It does not romanticize; it does not promise a second chance.

If requesting-to-remain could be read only at this level, then every Buddhist of every later generation reciting Samantabhadra's seventh vow would be reciting a mourning for an opportunity already lost.

But Huayan does not let this vow stop at mourning.

### Within One's Own Mind, Moment by Moment, a Buddha Is Attaining Perfect Awakening

On the Huayan assembly, the bodhisattva Samantabhadra gave another answer to the very same question.

The Chapter on the Manifestation of the Tathāgata (T0279, fascicle 52) contains a line that the three generations of Huayan patriarchs—Fazang, Chengguan, and Zongmi—cited again and again:

> Buddha-son, the bodhisattva-mahāsattva should know: **within one's own mind, moment by moment, a Buddha is always attaining perfect awakening.** Why is this? Because the Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, do not depart from this mind in order to attain perfect awakening. As with one's own mind, so too the minds of all sentient beings: all possess the Tathāgata attaining equal and perfect awakening, vast and all-pervading, present in every place, neither departing nor severed, without ever resting.

"Within one's own mind, moment by moment, a Buddha is always attaining perfect awakening."

⚠️ Read this line with care. It is all too easy to read it as subjective idealism—"the Buddha is only in my mind; outside there is no Buddha." That reading is wrong. The "own mind" of Huayan is not the "subjective mind" set against "external objects"; it is the **"dharma-realm mind" that exists before any subject-object opposition has even formed**. The "own" of "own mind" does not mean "mine privately"; it means "the dharma-realm's own self." The very next line of the scripture—"as with one's own mind, so too the minds of all sentient beings"—is precisely there to block this misreading: the sealing-function acts upon my mind and at the same time acts upon the minds of all sentient beings, because this "mind" is not private at all.

The same chapter contains eight more characters that are the core anchor of this chapter:

> Bodhi universally seals all mental activities, and therefore it is called perfect awakening.

These eight characters define "perfect awakening" directly as the very act of "universally sealing all mental activities." **Perfect awakening is not a state that has been attained; it is a sealing-function now in motion.**

"Universally" answers to the mutual containment of the myriad images—all mental activities, not one left out; "seals" answers to the precision with which the sealing-function necessarily appears as it acts upon every sentient being's every thought—a subtle correspondence, not one falling short of true. This is the scriptural origin-point of the profound gate this chapter answers to, the **gate of subtle inclusion and establishment**: *subtlety* is the precision of the universal-sealing function as it acts; *inclusion* is the non-obstruction of the myriad images appearing together; *establishment* is the stable self-standing of each one within that mutual inclusion.

The three operate at once, and perfect awakening is being sealed in this very moment.

### The Ocean Seal Is Not a Passive Mirror-Image

The first chapter of this book already cited the sixteen characters of Fazang's *Cessation of Delusion and Return to the Source*—"the original awakening of suchness; when delusion is exhausted and the mind clears, all the myriad images appear simultaneously." At that point the other face of the Ocean Seal had not yet been made explicit. This chapter supplies it: **the Ocean Seal is not a still mirror waiting for the myriad images to come and be reflected.**

"As a mirror reflects objects" is a simile often used in later explanations of the Ocean Seal, but the simile has a structural weakness: a mirror is static, passive, waiting for objects to arrive. If the Ocean Seal were only this, then requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world would degrade into "asking the mirror to keep being there"—a reading at once powerless and mistaken.

The verses of the Chapter on the Manifestation of the Tathāgata make the dynamic face of the Ocean Seal quite clear:

> Just as worlds undergo formation and destruction,
> Yet empty space neither increases nor decreases;
> All Buddhas emerge into the world,
> And the mark of bodhi is likewise.

The Ocean Seal is not an unchanging mirror-surface; the Ocean Seal is **that dimension within the flux of formation and destruction which neither increases nor decreases**. It is not stillness; it is motion that neither increases nor decreases.

Another passage:

> As with the sentient beings of the three times' kalpas and lands,
> All their mental activities and faculty-desires—
> Bodies equal in number to all of these are manifested;
> Therefore perfect awakening is called immeasurable.

The mental activities and faculty-desires of all sentient beings across the three times are infinite in number, and the Ocean Seal manifests a body to meet every one. The Ocean Seal **actively manifests itself**. Every mental activity of every sentient being is, in that very moment, answered by a corresponding bodily mark. "Manifest" here is a verb, not a noun.

Fazang says it directly in his *Search into the Profundity of the Avataṃsaka*:

> The gate of the perfectly bright Ocean Seal Samādhi: the root from which great functioning is creatively instituted.

The root from which great functioning is creatively instituted—the Ocean Seal is the foundation by which all great functioning can be set up, distinguished, and unfolded. It is not a mirror-image of the dharma-realm; it is the dharma-realm itself vibrating in this present moment.

What you are requesting is not a still mirror. What you are requesting is **this sealing-function itself—now vibrating, now manifesting, now responding**.

### Three-Time Radiant Manifestation: The Simultaneity of Time

Now we can return to the sharp question that opened this chapter—if the Ocean Seal truly is the sealing-function manifesting moment by moment, then why did Śākyamuni Buddha enter *nirvāṇa*?

The key to the answer lies in the sixth of the "ten meanings" by which Chengguan, in his *Commentary on the Avataṃsaka*, glosses the Ocean Seal: **three-time radiant manifestation**.

Three-time radiant manifestation describes how the function of the Ocean Seal handles the structure of time. The Ocean Seal does not manifest only "the present"; the Ocean Seal manifests past, present, and future at once—but "at once" carries a special sense here: **not cramming the three times into one and the same present, but letting present, past, and future each keep their own positions while all being sealed together.**

The "moment by moment" of "moment by moment a Buddha is always attaining perfect awakening" is the smallest unit of three-time radiant manifestation. Each single thought-moment is one radiant-manifestation point at which **past Buddhas have already attained perfect awakening, present Buddhas are now attaining perfect awakening, and future Buddhas will attain perfect awakening**—all at once.

These three tenses are not in sequence within the Ocean Seal; they are simultaneous.

Apply this structure to the question of the Cāpāla shrine and Kuśinagara:

Śākyamuni Buddha's physical body did pass away—this is a historical fact. It is continuously being sealed within the Ocean Seal; it does not vanish. But this fact **cannot obstruct the "a Buddha is attaining perfect awakening" of this present thought-moment from being sealed simultaneously alongside it**—this is exactly the structure of three-time radiant manifestation.

The āgama's "door of requesting" is a one-time, time-bounded event-threshold—the opportunity of a single historical moment. Huayan's "door of requesting" is raised, through the three-time radiant manifestation of the Ocean Seal, into a **structural door present in every thought-moment**.

This reversal is not Huayan negating the āgama. The rain-drenched night at the Cāpāla shrine did occur; Ānanda was indeed silent three times; Śākyamuni did enter *nirvāṇa* at Kuśinagara—Huayan denies none of these facts. **Huayan only replaces these facts within the temporal structure of the Ocean Seal**, so that "the past Buddha entering *nirvāṇa*" and "the present Buddha remaining in the world" are sealed simultaneously within the Ocean Seal.

⚠️ One necessary correction. Acknowledgment does not cancel history. Huayan does not deny Śākyamuni Buddha's passing—he did pass away; his physical body is indeed no longer in this world. Huayan only says: this fact of "already passed away" and the structural fact that within one's own mind a Buddha is moment by moment attaining perfect awakening **are sealed at the same time within the Ocean Seal**. The two facts do not conflict—one is the historical layer, the other the structural layer. The acknowledgment of Vow Seven is an acknowledgment of the structural layer; it does not rewrite the historical layer.

This is also why you still can, and still should, go on reciting the Buddha's name, bowing to the Buddha, and chanting the scriptures—these acts are, at the historical layer, a remembrance of the Śākyamuni Buddha who has passed away, and at the structural layer an acknowledgment of the sealing-function present moment by moment. **Both layers hold at once**, and neither needs to be cancelled.

### Vow Seven Is Not Only Petition but Acknowledgment

The conclusions of the preceding sections can be gathered into one sentence: **Vow Seven is not only petition; it is also acknowledgment.**

What you request is not only that some external Buddha extend the lifespan of his physical body—of the Buddhas and Tathāgatas of the ten directions and three times who are about to manifest *parinirvāṇa*, the sūtra plainly bids you make earnest entreaty, and that layer stands word for word. Deeper still, what you acknowledge is the structural fact that within your own mind, moment by moment, a Buddha is always attaining perfect awakening. What remains is not only Śākyamuni Buddha's physical form; it is also this sealing-function itself. And this sealing-function **remains moment by moment and has never departed**.

The true action of the four words "requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world" is not only asking some external thing to change its state; it is also **turning the mind toward the sealing-function that has been at work all along**.

This "acknowledgment" is not the affirmation of a philosophical proposition; it is a present, actual act—just as, in the instant the king of the Brahmā heaven knew from afar, there was no calculation, no effort, no preparation; the distant knowing was already, in itself, complete. Acknowledgment is the same: it is not first thinking and then doing; it is an act in itself.

### Practice: Three Scenes from the Daily Round

The three scenes below show how the three marks of Vow Seven—subtlety, inclusion, establishment—operate simultaneously in the present moment of everyday practice.

**Scene One · The First Thought on Waking**

The first thought on waking is often scattered—bodily sensation, the residue of last night's dreams, the day's errands, the leftover breakfast in the fridge, all pouring out in a tangle. The usual antidote is "do not let thought arise" or "clear it out the moment it arises," but both antidotes set thought and awareness in opposition.

The practice of Vow Seven is different. At the very moment the first thought pours out, gently acknowledge: "Within this thought, a Buddha is attaining perfect awakening."

**Subtlety**—not imagining the first thought to be "this is the Buddha," but acknowledging that the function of the Ocean Seal is universally sealing it with a precision that corresponds to this scattered thought.

**Inclusion**—the scattered thought and the thought "a Buddha is attaining perfect awakening" do not obstruct each other in this present moment. They are two faces of the same sealing-function, not two things that must exclude each other.

**Establishment**—the scattered thought is still in its position, not eliminated; "a Buddha is attaining perfect awakening" is also in its position, not petitioned over from outside. Both stand stably in their places.

This is the present-moment practice of requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world. The Buddha has not come from outside—because the Buddha has never departed; the scattered thought has not been negated—because the scattered thought is itself the object being universally sealed.

**Scene Two · The Moment of Bowing to the Buddha**

Bowing to the Buddha is among the most universal daily practices in Chinese Buddhism. The common reading is "I bow to the Buddha; the Buddha is over there"—the one who bows on this side, the one bowed to on that side, the act of homage in between.

The reading of Vow Seven inverts this structure.

**Subtlety**—every bodily motion of the bow (joining the palms, bending, bringing the head to the ground) is being universally sealed by the function of the Ocean Seal; the major and minor marks of the Buddha are being universally sealed at the same time.

**Inclusion**—the one who bows and the one bowed to are not two opposed positions within the Ocean Seal; they are two entry-points of the same sealing-function. "The one who pays homage and the one who receives it—their natures are empty and still": this is exactly the doctrine here.

**Establishment**—the act of bowing is still the act of bowing; the Buddha is still the Buddha. The concrete act of homage keeps its full integrity at the level of phenomena; "inclusion" does not cancel "establishment."

The concrete action of requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world within the bow is this: before, during, and after the bow, silently acknowledge in the heart, "Within this one bow, the Buddhas are moment by moment attaining perfect awakening"—and this acknowledgment is itself the request. It is not an extra petition-thought added on; it is reading the act of bowing itself as the request.

**Scene Three · Facing a Person Who Wrongs You**

The third scene is the hardest—facing someone who is hurting you, misunderstanding you, making aversion rise in your heart.

The usual antidotes are "cultivate patience," "contemplate them as a parent from a past life," "contemplate the emptiness of dharmas"—all effective antidotes, but each requires first pressing down the present aversion.

The practice of Vow Seven does not require first pressing down aversion. At the very moment aversion arises, gently acknowledge: "This person's mental activity is also within the universal sealing of the Ocean Seal."

**Subtlety**—the function of the Ocean Seal has a corresponding seal for this person's harming mental activity in this moment, and a corresponding seal for your aversion in this moment. Each of the two mental activities is universally sealed with its own corresponding precision; neither is "left out."

**Inclusion**—harming and being harmed, the one who harms and the one harmed, are not opposed poles within the Ocean Seal; they are two positions at which the same sealing-function is acting.

**Establishment**—the harm is still harm, and the worldly judgment is not cancelled; the aversion is still aversion, and your own feeling is not denied. But beneath this "still" there is now one more layer—an acknowledgment. This acknowledgment does not change the event, but it changes your relation to the event.

This practice is not telling you to forgive at once, to let go at once—those are later fruits. It tells you to do only this, in the present: to acknowledge—**this person's mental activity, in this moment, is also being universally sealed by the Ocean Seal; a Buddha is attaining perfect awakening here, in this very place, now.** The acknowledgment is itself requesting the Buddhas to remain in the world. The appearance of a person who wrongs you is, within Huayan doctrine, one concrete door at which Vow Seven can be practiced.

⚠️ One methodological self-limitation: the three scenes above are only demonstrations; they **do not replace the foundational path-factors built up in the earlier parts of this book**. Without the foundation of discipline, concentration, and wisdom, "acknowledgment" alone is not enough—it will slide into "meditation sickness" or "empty talk of perfect fusion." The posture of acknowledgment presupposes that you already have the foundational path-factors and are only adding, upon that foundation, one further layer of Huayan insight. Neither can be done without.

### Closing

Here the chapter rests.

Ānanda's three silences let one historical opportunity be lost forever. Huayan does not go chasing after that lost opportunity—**Huayan opens the door in another dimension**. In that dimension the door is not a one-time event but a structure present in every thought-moment; not a request external to you, but the present acknowledgment that within your own mind, moment by moment, a Buddha is always attaining perfect awakening.

The sealing-function of the original awakening of suchness is present moment by moment and has never departed. It is not an object you must call back—it has never gone far. All you need to do is, in this one thought, this one bow, this one moment of facing a person who wrongs you, turn the mind toward the sealing-function that has been at work all along. Acknowledgment is itself the act.

The next chapter pushes this acknowledgment into its next natural movement—**following**. If the Buddha is present moment by moment, then what you follow is no longer the footsteps of some Buddha in history but the moment-by-moment sealing-function itself. The eighth chapter takes the gate of the mutual containment of one and many without difference as its window, paired with Samantabhadra's eighth vow—constantly following the Buddha in study. From acknowledgment (Vow Seven) to following (Vow Eight), the whole structure of Huayan cultivation and realization enters its unfolding through the perfect fusion of the six characteristics.

> Every chapter of this series stops somewhere. The place where it stops is not an oversight; it is reverence. A three-dimensional pen cannot write the ten-dimensional object; all it can do is mark clearly the place where the pen is set down—so that the reader knows that, past this line, one must rely on one's own dharma-eye.

This chapter's pen rests here.
