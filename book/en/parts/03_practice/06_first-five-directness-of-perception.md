## 3.6 The First Five Consciousnesses: The Immediacy of the Senses

Chapter 3.5 dealt with the sixth, the mind-consciousness—the translator who can look in the mirror. But where does the translator get its raw material to translate? It comes from the very front line of the eight consciousnesses, from **five windows**: eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body consciousness.

These five, taken together, are called **the first five consciousnesses**. On the map of the eight consciousnesses they are the layer that lies closest to the outer world—the direct receivers of form, sound, scent, taste, and touch. At first glance they look like simple couriers: they present whatever they see, relay whatever they hear, and hand the raw material over to the mind-consciousness for discrimination and judgment.

This understanding is only half right.

The other side of the first five consciousnesses is that they are **the very entrance to the whole assembly line of suffering**. In the Āgamas the Buddha teaches a chain of dependent arising again and again:

> Conditioned by the eye and form, eye consciousness arises; the conjunction of these three produces contact; conditioned by contact, feeling; **conditioned by feeling, craving**; conditioned by craving, clinging; conditioned by clinging, becoming... thus arises the entire mass of suffering.

—Saṃyukta Āgama, fascicle 8, Sūtra 218

From the eye seeing a flower (eye consciousness), to the contact of the conjunction of three (contact), to the sensation (a pleasant feeling), to "I like this" (the "craving" here is the Pali *taṇhā*—craving, thirst, not romantic love in the ordinary sense), to grasping and possessing (clinging), to creating karma (becoming)—along the whole assembly line of suffering, **the first link is the conjunction of three at the first five consciousnesses**.

So the first five consciousnesses are double-edged: **through the same window flows both the entrance to suffering and the entrance to practice**.

### Real Objects, Direct Perception, Pervading Three Natures—The Cognition Closest to Purity

The first verse: "Real objects, direct perception, pervading three moral natures." These seven words describe the essential nature of the first five consciousnesses.

**Real objects**: what they apprehend is the true intrinsic character of the object, free of verbal concepts. The red that eye consciousness sees is simply the intrinsic character of red; the sound that ear consciousness hears is simply the intrinsic character of sound. There is no verbal label "this is red" or "this is a bird call"—those are added later by the mind-consciousness.

**Direct perception**: presented directly, without inference, without recollection. The first five consciousnesses operate only in the single instant when the object is present before them—the moment the form-dust passes, eye consciousness comes to rest. They have no ability to "recall what just happened" (that is the mind-consciousness's discrimination by recollection), and no ability to "infer the future" (that is the mind-consciousness's discrimination by calculation).

**Pervading three natures**: they can be wholesome, unwholesome, or neutral. This point is especially important—it means **the first five consciousnesses have a moral dimension**; they are not purely mechanical sensors. Seeing the same person, eye consciousness driven by compassion and eye consciousness driven by aversion are both direct perception, yet they differ in quality. The wholesome or unwholesome, the defiled or pure, of the first five consciousnesses comes from what they rest upon—manas and the eighth consciousness. This is exactly why the inner transformation of 3.3 and 3.4 conducts all the way down to your eyes and ears at this very moment.

Set this verdict against the other consciousnesses and it becomes clear: the ālaya-vijñāna apprehends only real objects, only direct perception, only the unobscured-neutral (no moral dimension); manas apprehends only composite objects, only non-veridical cognition, only the obscured-neutral (a structural error); the sixth consciousness ranges across all three objects, all three modes, all three natures (pervading all, but mixed with all). **Only the first five consciousnesses combine "real objects and direct perception" with "a moral dimension."** Among the eight consciousnesses, this is the mode of cognition closest to pure direct perception.

### Nine Conditions for Consciousness-Arising, Three by Contact and Two at Distance—Dependent Arising Shown Concretely

The second verse: "All five alike depend on pure form faculties; nine conditions, seven and eight—close neighbors in their arising; three by contact, two at distance—thus they observe the dusty world."

These three lines describe how the first five consciousnesses arise. They cannot just appear at will—they require the convergence of nine conditions.

Eye consciousness requires **nine conditions**: space (distance), illumination (light), faculty (the pure form faculty), object (form-dust), attention, the discriminating support (the sixth consciousness), the defiled/pure support (manas), the root support (the ālaya-vijñāna), and seed. Ear consciousness needs one fewer—it does not need "illumination," for one can hear sound in the dark, so eight conditions suffice. Nose, tongue, and body consciousness need one fewer still—they do not need "space" (there must be contact for perception), so seven conditions are enough.

This is "nine conditions, seven and eight." Then "three by contact, two at distance"—nose, tongue, and body consciousness must **make contact** with their objects to function (the tongue must touch the food), while eye and ear consciousness must stand **at a distance** from their objects to function (press your eye against the page and you can no longer read it).

These details are not an exercise in memorizing terms. They point to one fundamental insight: **the first five consciousnesses never operate independently.** Behind every act of seeing, every act of hearing, nine conditions (or seven, or eight) converge at once—including, note well, the seeds of the ālaya-vijñāna, the defilement and purity of manas, the discrimination of the sixth consciousness. You suppose you "purely see" a flower, but in fact the whole eight-consciousness system is operating at the same moment.

This is the concrete display, at the level of the eight consciousnesses, of 2.4's "illuminating the five aggregates as empty": the first five consciousnesses are wholly dependent arising—there is no independent "one who sees" seated behind them.

### Sense-Faculty Restraint—The Single Thought-Moment Between Direct Perception and Calculation

Where is the space for practice in the first five consciousnesses? It is in **the gap** between them and the mind-consciousness.

The Buddha gave an extremely precise teaching for this, called **sense-faculty restraint**:

> When the eye sees form, do not grasp at the sign, do not grasp at the secondary characteristics.
> —Saṃyukta Āgama, fascicle 11, Sūtra 275

⚠️ There is a common misreading here: to suppose that "not grasping at the sign" means closing the eyes and not looking, plugging the ears and not listening, shutting the senses down. **It does not.** What the Buddha taught is "skillfully guarding the sense-gates"—not letting the habit of grasping at signs activate even as you look, not refusing to look. The turtle-withdrawing-its-limbs simile is often misread as "permanently closing the six faculties"; but the perfect-penetration methods of the Śūraṅgama Sūtra are clearer—Aniruddha lost his physical eyes yet "the essential truth shone through, like observing a fruit in my palm," and the Avataṃsaka Sūtra speaks of "entering right samādhi within the eye-faculty, emerging from samādhi within the form-realm." Faculty and object are not obstacles; they are the very ground for entering and leaving samādhi.

At which instant does practice intervene? Return to the assembly line of suffering: **eye consciousness sees → the conjunction of three produces contact → feeling → craving.**

The first three steps are an automatic process at the level of the first five consciousnesses—pure direct perception, which at the stage of an ordinary being can hardly be interfered with at all. But from "feeling" to "craving"—from sensation to "I like it / I dislike it"—there lies an extremely subtle gap. This gap is the point of intervention for the mind-consciousness that 3.5 described; but it **must be caught at the live scene of sensory contact**, not handled afterward in recollection. The work of sense-faculty restraint is to station the power of awareness inside this gap.

Take an everyday example. You walk into a coffee house, the door has just opened, and nose consciousness instantly receives the aroma of coffee (direct perception, real object, wholly pure)—that is the first step. The conjunction of three (nose faculty, scent-dust, nose consciousness) produces contact—that is the second step. Contact produces a pleasant feeling—that is the third step, still within the automatic process of the first five consciousnesses and the five-concomitant mind-consciousness.

The trouble begins from the next instant. The independent mind-consciousness intervenes, discrimination by calculation switches on—"I'd love a cup," "today I'll treat myself to something special," "this place is nice, I should come often." **In that single instant between "pleasant feeling" and "I want"**, the whole assembly line of suffering begins to turn. Sense-faculty restraint does not make you unable to smell the coffee (not smelling it would mean the senses are broken), nor does it force you not to order (that is suppression). Rather, in the very instant the pleasant feeling arises, it is to **be aware that "right now, a pleasant feeling is present"**—and then whether or not to take the next step becomes a genuinely open choice, no longer the automatic execution of habit.

This gap looks, on the surface, like a fraction of a second; in substance it is the distance between an ordinary being and a sage.

⚠️ Another misreading: to suppose that an ordinary being's direct perception by the first five consciousnesses is the same as "knowing and seeing things as they truly are." **It is not.** Though the first five consciousnesses are direct perception, they are defiled by the habit-energies of beginningless ignorance—an ordinary being's eye consciousness and a sage's eye consciousness, looking at the same flower, do not present the same thing. Pure direct perception is genuinely realized only at the sage's stage. Do not equate "I directly saw it" with "I saw it as it truly is"—between the two still lies the whole distance of the path.

### The Wisdom of Accomplishing What Must Be Done—The Sudden Turn at the Vajra-like Samādhi

The third verse: "When perfect luminosity first arises, they become non-outflow; three classes of emanation-bodies bring the wheel of suffering to rest."

The transformed destination of the first five consciousnesses is **the wisdom of accomplishing what must be done**—for the sake of benefiting and gladdening sentient beings, manifesting throughout the ten directions every kind of transformation of the three karmas. No longer the courier who passively receives form, sound, scent, taste, and touch, but the spiritual emissary who actively manifests form, sound, scent, taste, and touch.

This transformation has a distinctive feature: **it is a sudden turn, not a gradual one.** Chapter 3.5 described how the sixth consciousness's transformation is a thirteen-ground journey advancing step by step through the first ground, the fourth, the seventh, the eighth. But the first five consciousnesses are different—they must wait until the very instant the **vajra-like samādhi** (the final uninterrupted-path thought-moment of the bodhisattva at the stage of virtual enlightenment, just before Buddhahood) appears. As the ālaya-vijñāna turns into the great perfect mirror wisdom and the five faculties turn into non-outflow pure form faculties, **only then do the first five consciousnesses leap, in a single instant, from outflow into non-outflow.**

Why so? Because the first five consciousnesses depend on the form faculties. The form faculties are the image-component of the ālaya-vijñāna; if the ālaya does not turn, the form faculties cannot become non-outflow; if the form faculties are not non-outflow, the first five consciousnesses cannot become non-outflow. Link by link, they turn together at the last. This is the most dramatic scene in the whole of Yogācāra practice—**in a single thought-moment, the first five consciousnesses pass from ordinary being to Buddha.**
