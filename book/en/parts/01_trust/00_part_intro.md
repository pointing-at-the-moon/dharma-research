# Part One · Trust · Initial Aspiration

This book begins with trust. Not trust in the religious sense—not being asked to believe in anything—but something more fundamental: **generating the aspiration**.

You opened this book, perhaps out of curiosity, perhaps out of confusion, perhaps only because someone recommended it. Whatever the reason, you are already doing something more important than you realize: you have turned your attention inward. In the language of the Dharma, this is where trust begins—not being certain of the answer, but being certain the question is worth asking.

Trust, understand, practice, inquiry, cutting, illumination, gathering-in, manifestation—eight words, one road. Trust is the first step, because without trust there is no setting out at all. But trust is not the destination; trust only pushes the door open. What you see once it opens, how you walk, where you arrive—that is the business of the seven parts that follow.

---

What this part sets out to answer is a question that sounds small but is in fact very large: **why does it matter that a person generates the aspiration to practice? Why begin here, and not somewhere else?**

On the surface, generating the aspiration is just a thought rising in the mind—wanting to practice, wanting to change, wanting to walk a road different from yesterday's. Compared with reading a sutra, keeping a precept, or sitting through one stick of incense, it looks like the lightest, softest, least substantial thing of all. Most people, over a lifetime, raise countless such thoughts—they rise and fade, fade and rise, and in the end nothing seems to come of them.

Buddhism's verdict on this is exactly the reverse of common sense. **Generating the aspiration is not the lightest thing; it is the starting point that makes the whole road a road.** Without this single thought, the understanding, practice, inquiry, cutting, illumination, gathering-in, and manifestation that follow have no way to occur at all—you have not even taken the first step, and the scenery of the seven parts that follow has nothing to do with you. With this single thought, even if at this moment you can do nothing and understand nothing, in that very instant the entire dharma-realm has already responded to your direction.

This also answers the question of "why begin with trust, and not with understanding." Understanding, with no trust beneath it, becomes a hoarding of knowledge; practice, with no trust beneath it, becomes a repetition of forms. Trust is the foundation that lets all seven of the parts that follow stand up.

---

But saying "it matters that you begin here" is not enough. The step has to be walked all the way through, its inner structure brought out, so that you see why this single thought can bear the weight of the whole road that follows. The ten chapters of Part One are that step walked through to the end.

It begins with **a single aspiration** (1.1)—a chapter whose aim is not the sermon that "generating the aspiration is a good thing," but a harder proposition: this single thought rising in your mind right now sends ripples through the entire shared-karmic field along a strict causal structure. The butterfly effect is not a metaphor; it is a precise description of Yogācāra causation. Then comes the **Dharma-flow** (1.2)—this thought can be roused at all not because you produced it out of nothing, but because the Dharma-flow of the Buddhas of the three times passes through you in this moment. You think it is you generating the aspiration; in truth a node within the all-pervading dharma-body has just been lit. Chapters 1.1 and 1.2 are the two cornerstones of Part One—one facing outward, where generating the aspiration affects the dharma-realm; one facing inward, where the dharma-realm pours into the aspiration. Together they establish the question of "why generating the aspiration matters."

Once that is established, the question turns naturally: **and what about our own age?** In 2026, everyone holds in their hand a tool without precedent. Where does that tool sit on the map of Yogācāra? Chapters 1.3 through 1.5 are a set of three that locate the AI age—1.3 makes clear that **an LLM is not a consciousness**; it generates no karma, produces no merit, has no ālaya-vijñāna. 1.4 is the refutation—**mistaking the tool for a seed**, the contemporary wrong view, and why the logic of "getting rich through AI" is mistaken at the root. 1.5 is the establishment—since AI is not a direct cause, how it should be used rightly as a **contributing condition**. These three chapters foreshadow Part Four, "inquiry," but they must be handled here in Part One first—without pinning down the coordinates of the age, every theory of practice that follows would be dragged off course by the questions of the age.

Then come three chapters on more inward mechanisms. 1.6, **awareness is the turning**, takes up the mental factor of "attention" (one of the five universal factors) and tells you where in body and mind the single most decisive thought of practice takes place. 1.7, **seeds and fruit**, pries open the causal structure inside the ālaya-vijñāna and answers the most common question of all—"can doing good cancel out the bad?" 1.8, **seeking outward is a dead-end road walked to its end**, takes up a more fundamental question: since satisfaction is not out there, why do people keep searching outward? These three chapters unfold the inner machinery of generating the aspiration, so that you see how a single thought of mind actually works.

By 1.9, a practical question blocks the way—you have understood these principles, but can you receive them? The chapter on the **dharma-vessel** diagnoses the attention-injury of the digital age and gives the entry point for repair. Without this chapter, the principles of the first eight would leak right through you.

Last is 1.10, the **thirty-seven factors of awakening**. Three months before the Buddha entered final nirvāṇa, he gathered the conclusion of a lifetime's teaching into these thirty-seven items—not thirty-seven separate things, but thirty-seven faces of one and the same project of awakening. This chapter takes all the modern self-help books—*Atomic Habits*, *Peak*, *Flow*, *Grit*—and sets them back against it, so that you see the complete methodology already prepared two thousand five hundred years ago.

---

Ten chapters, ten angles. From the butterfly effect to the thirty-seven factors of awakening, from the questions out there back to the mechanisms within. When you have walked this part through, you will know one thing: the thing in your hands—practice—is no small matter. This single aspiration you generate now, seen in hindsight, is the starting point of the whole road.

But the starting point is not the road. The next chapter begins with that most central thought—what, exactly, that thought has done in you.
