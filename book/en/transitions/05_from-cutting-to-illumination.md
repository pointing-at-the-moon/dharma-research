## Transition ⑤ · From Cutting to Illumination

The Diamond Sūtra has finished cutting.

Ten strokes, brought down one after another—appearance, dharma, merit, the dwelling of the mind, the practitioner's self, the Buddha's physical body, all that is conditioned—every one of them cut through. The blade is set down, and the hand is empty.

But the empty hand has a new problem at once.

What you just cut through—did the cut land true?

---

Cutting is an action. To perform that action you must already know where the target is.

From the first chapter of Part V through the tenth, the diamond blade's target was always clear—appearance, dharma, merit, the dwelling of the mind; every stroke had its definite object. But what if the target itself was mistaken? What if the "appearance" you cut into was not the real appearance, but only a posture that imagined itself to be cutting appearance?

When the target is wrong, the sharper the blade, the further off the cut.

What is needed now is a different organ—not a blade, but a mirror.

---

⚠️ A middle-path correction has to come first here: the Śūraṅgama is not deeper than the Diamond.

Blade and mirror are two different organs doing two different jobs. The blade is for action; the mirror is for diagnosis. However sharp the diamond blade, if it cuts the wrong object it cuts further and further astray; however clear the Śūraṅgama mirror, if it has no cutting force behind it, then even what it sees it cannot move. Neither one can be done without.

The first five parts honed the blade. Part VI now sets out to polish the mirror.

---

The Śūraṅgama mirror reflects a very precise kind of object—the practitioner's own self-deception.

The Diamond Sūtra cuts attachments out in the open—attachment to appearance, attachment to the teaching, attachment to merit. These attachments are obvious; any eye that has prajñā can pick them out. The Śūraṅgama Sūtra reflects what lies in the dark—the self-deceptions that the practitioner does not even know are there. Seeing light in meditation, sensing a strange fragrance, suddenly "getting through" a little, feeling that you have "arrived somewhere"—every one of these may be a subtler layer of conceptual construction. The blade can find no purchase at this level, because it does not know what to cut. Only when the mirror reflects the practitioner's own self back can you see that small step where you suppose you are awakening while in fact you are veering off course.

So the famous "fifty skandha-māras" of the Śūraṅgama Sūtra are not a danger list for readers to steer around—they are a diagnostic manual, asking the reader to hold each one up against the self. The fifty skandha-māras are not fifty ways other people go astray; they are fifty **ways you may already have gone astray without yet noticing**. The right posture for reading these fifty is not "I would never do that," but "have I already done that?"

The work of the mirror, from first to last, is this one sentence.

---

The thirteen chapters of Part VI are three directions for using the same one mirror.

First comes **verifying the mind**—the seven places where the mind is sought, the ten revelations of seeing, the four categories and seven elements—checking whether the "mind" you take for granted is really where you take it to be. Then comes **selecting the method**—the twenty-five means of perfect penetration, finding the one path of practice that suits you, fitted with the three gradual stages and bounded by the four clear instructions on purity. Last comes **discerning the māras**—the fifty skandha-māras sorted into the five layers of form, feeling, perception, formation, and consciousness, laying every possible self-deception of practice out in the open daylight.

These are not three stages; they are three angles of the same one mirror. By the end of these thirteen chapters, the reader will leave with a new habit—at every instant of feeling you have "gained something," to lift that "something gained" up before the mirror and take a look first. This habit matters more than memorizing the name of every one of the fifty skandha-māras.

Before entering this part, set the diamond blade down for the time being. The mirror is not for hacking with.
